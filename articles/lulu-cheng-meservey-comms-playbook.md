## 1. 核心世界观：Go Direct（直接发声）
## 1. Core Worldview: Go Direct

**中文**
"Go direct" 不是"开个 Twitter 账号"，而是有具体定义的：必须由创始人或高管本人、用第一人称、用真实的人的口吻说话，让人看到你的失误和脆弱，并成为社区的"大使"。如果账号背后是一家"公司"，那就不是直接渠道。

她给出"进攻"和"防守"两个理由：
- **防守**：创业公司天然是反叛者，会被攻击，没有大机构替你辩护，只能自己站出来。而且要提前"预热市场"，让受众习惯你的发声节奏，否则平时不说话、出事才发声，别人会以为你出了危机。
- **进攻**：如果你做的事真的新，没有任何记者能比你讲得更好，因为他们理解得没你深。

**English**
"Going direct" is not "open a Twitter account." She defines it precisely: a founder or senior executive speaks for themselves, in the first person, in a human voice, visibly making mistakes and being vulnerable, and acts as an ambassador to the community. If the other side of the channel is a corporation, there is no direct connection.

She gives an offense and a defense rationale:
- **Defense**: startups are insurgents and will be attacked; no institution will defend you, so you must build the channel yourself. And you must "prime the market" so your audience is used to your cadence, otherwise silence followed by sudden posting reads as a crisis.
- **Offense**: if what you do is truly new, no reporter can tell it as well as you, because they do not understand it as well as you.

## 2. 让想法传播：把药片藏进奶酪里
## 2. Making Ideas Spread: Put the Pill in the Cheese

**中文**
她对"想法如何传播"的总原则：必须**易记**，并且让人**自愿**去复述。没人愿意免费替公司做宣传，但人们愿意逗别人笑、显得有趣、或表达自己的身份认同。所以要给想法穿上便于传播的外衣：
1. 变成一句能被复述的段子或口号（"move fast and break things"、"don't be evil"、"build something people want"）；
2. 用类比；
3. 造一个鲜明的画面（她自己的例子："put the pill in some cheese"，喂狗吃药要藏在奶酪里）；
4. 用故事和轶事，不要用形容词，因为形容词太主观，对听者毫无意义。

打造口号的具体方法：把公司或使命浓缩成一句话 → 改写成二年级小学生能懂的版本 → 剔除所有陈词滥调 → 尽量转成类比或画面。核心是**把接收者的认知负担降到最低**。

**English**
Her general principle for spreading ideas: it has to be **memorable**, and people have to **want** to repeat it of their own volition. Nobody repeats things to do a corporation a favor; they do it to make someone laugh, to seem interesting, or to signal identity. So dress the idea for travel:
1. Turn it into a joke or repeatable line ("move fast and break things," "don't be evil," "build something people want");
2. Use an analogy;
3. Create a vivid mental image (her own: "put the pill in some cheese," as you would to get a dog to swallow medicine);
4. Use a story or anecdote instead of adjectives, because adjectives are subjective and meaningless to the listener.

Her recipe for a sticky phrase: boil the company or mission down to one sentence → rewrite it so a second-grader gets it → cleanse it of clichés → turn it into an analogy or an image. The goal is to **minimize the cognitive burden on the recipient**.

**引用 / Quotes**

> "You have to make it memorable and you have to make people want to say it of their own volition."
> 「你得让它易记，还得让人自愿地说出口。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:05:03

> "Use a story, use an anecdote instead of using adjectives because adjectives are so subjective, they're meaningless to people."
> 「用故事、用轶事，别用形容词，因为形容词太主观，对人来说毫无意义。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:05:47

> "You want to make it something that a second-grader could understand. You want to minimize the cognitive burden on the recipient."
> 「要让二年级小学生都能听懂。要把接收者的认知负担降到最低。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:07:35

**案例 A / Case A: "Binders full of women"（反面教材）**
中文：2012 年美国大选辩论中罗姆尼说出"binders of women"，一夜之间病毒式传播，因为它画面感强、措辞怪异、极易被玩梗。她用这个例子说明画面感的威力，同时警告：这种机制也会反噬你。
English: Mitt Romney's "binders of women" line from the 2012 debate spread like wildfire because you can picture the binder, the phrasing is unusual, and it lends itself to memes. She uses it to show the power of imagery, and as a warning that the same mechanism can backfire on you.

**案例 B / Case B: 她自己的失败推文——"neither, for that matter, is the sea"**
中文：Substack 因"审核不够"被围攻时，她在产假期间发了一条支持言论自由立场的推文串，整体反响很好（她记得约三万多个赞，且来自目标人群：在网上写作的人）。但其中有一条写着"这样做并不愉快，但话说回来，大海也不愉快"——这是她刚读的《纽约客》书评里的一个诗意典故。结果那条推文的点赞断崖式下跌，读者一脸"你在说什么"。她的复盘：**把只有自己懂的梗放出去，是大忌。**
English: When Substack was under fire for "not censoring enough," she posted a thread defending its free-expression stance while on maternity leave. The thread traveled well (she recalls 30-something thousand likes from the audience that mattered: people who write online). But one tweet said something like "doing this isn't pleasant, but neither, for that matter, is the sea," a poetic reference from a New Yorker book review she had just read. Likes on that one tweet dropped off a cliff and readers asked what she was talking about. Her lesson: **never release an inside joke with yourself into the world.**

> "I did the thing you're not supposed to do, which is take an inside joke with yourself and release it into the world."
> 「我做了那件不该做的事：把一个只有自己懂的梗放到了世界上。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:09:53

**案例 C / Case C: "Cultural erogenous zones" 这个短语本身**
中文：她反过来举了一个成功的例子：她常说"找到受众的 cultural erogenous zones（文化敏感带）"。这个短语一听就懂、不是私人梗、又足够怪异让人想复述。这就是"药片藏进奶酪"的正面示范。
English: As a positive counterexample she cites her own phrase, "find your audience's cultural erogenous zones." You instantly know what it means, it is not a private joke, and it is unusual enough that you want to repeat it. That is the pill-in-cheese principle working.

## 3. 文化敏感带：搭一座从 X 到 Y 的桥
## 3. Cultural Erogenous Zones: Build the Bridge from X to Y

**中文**
人们在乎什么、不在乎什么，你改变不了。试图改变别人的世界观是"重活"，把你的东西塑造成符合他们已有热情的样子是"轻活"。如果你的自然受众在乎 X，而你提供的是 Y，你的工作就是**造一个 API，把 X 连到 Y**。她说自己一辈子没见过谁能靠一个故事让完全不在乎的人突然变得热情——所以别指望"造出来就有人来"。

同时要承认：有些人就不是你的受众，别浪费时间。

**English**
People either care about something or they don't, and you won't change that. Changing someone's worldview is a huge lift; shaping your thing to fit their existing passions is a light lift. If your natural audience cares about X and you offer Y, your job is to **build the API, the bridge from X to Y**. She says she has never in her life seen someone craft a story so powerful that a person who didn't care suddenly made it their passion, so "build it and they will come" does not apply to messaging.

Also accept that some people are simply not your audience. Don't waste time on them.

**引用 / Quotes**

> "If your natural audience cares about X and you're offering Y, then it's your job to create the API or to create the bridge from X to Y."
> 「如果你的自然受众在乎 X，而你提供的是 Y，那你的工作就是造一个 API，搭一座从 X 到 Y 的桥。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:15:26

> "It's a huge lift to try to change someone's worldview or their passions. It's a light lift to take the thing you want to talk about and just shape it... to fit into their worldview."
> 「改变别人的世界观或热情是重活；把你想说的东西塑造成符合他们世界观的样子是轻活。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:14:54

**案例 A / Case A: Kamala Harris 把 K-12 教育嫁接到国防**
中文：她最爱举的例子（她强调与政治立场无关）：Harris 竞选参议员时，K-12 教育没人关心，但国家安全人人关心。于是她对关心国防的人说："你知道参军需要十年级阅读水平吗？读不懂陆军野战手册就当不了兵。所以你若关心能否维持常备军，就必须关心初中阅读标准。"这条桥搭得如此漂亮，以至于多年后 Lulu 还在复述。
English: Her favorite example (she stresses it has nothing to do with politics): when Kamala Harris ran for Senate, nobody cared about K-12 education, but everyone cared about national security. So she told the national-security crowd: did you know you need a 10th-grade reading level to enlist, because otherwise you can't read the Army field manual? If you care about maintaining a standing army, you must care about middle-school reading standards. The bridge was so good that Lulu is still repeating it years later.

**案例 B / Case B: Substack 反击记者时的框架选择**
中文：Substack 几次不得不公开反驳记者的不实说法。她的"私心"当然是为公司正名，但她清楚："有记者说了 Substack 坏话"没几个人在乎；而"言论自由、新闻自由、建立自己媒体平台的权利"有大量人深切在乎。所以反击永远框在后者上。这也是她在"文化敏感带"里的直接应用。
English: Substack several times had to push back publicly against unfair or untrue claims by journalists. Her ulterior motive was to vindicate the company, but she knew that "a journalist said something mean about Substack" is something few people care about, while free expression, press freedom, and the right to build your own media platform are things many people care about deeply. So the pushback was always framed on the latter. That is the erogenous-zone principle applied to a fight.

> "Not a lot of people care that a journalist said something mean about Substack."
> 「没多少人在乎某个记者说了 Substack 的坏话。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:27:15

## 4. 同心圆：不能跳过任何一圈
## 4. Concentric Circles: You Can't Skip a Circle

**中文**
弱势方（underdog）传播的核心方法：**从自己的办公桌开始，按同心圆向外扩散**。
- 第 0 圈：你自己。先把信息说清楚，从脑中 1000 个事实里只挑 1 到 3 个。这很难，因为你知道得太多。
- 第 1 圈：几乎永远是员工。
- 往外：联合创始人、高管、投资人、重度用户、行业意见领袖、报道该领域的媒体……顺序视公司类型而定，按"对你成败的影响力"和"其他人赋予他们的可信度"排序。

为什么必须按顺序？因为**每一圈都会假设内圈比自己更懂，并跟随内圈的态度**。如果员工不兴奋，外界会想："他们最清楚，他们都不兴奋，我们为什么要兴奋？"更危险的是，早期员工如果感到"货不对板"而心怀不满，他就变成了一个极可信、和你同一个社交圈、还想毁掉你的人。

**English**
Her core method for underdogs: **spread outward in concentric circles starting from your own desk**.
- Circle 0: yourself. Get crisp; pick 1 to 3 facts out of the 1,000 in your head. This is hard precisely because you know too much.
- Circle 1: almost always employees.
- Outward: co-founders, executives, investors, power users, category influencers, the reporters who cover your space… The order depends on the company, ranked by how much each group can influence your success and how much credibility other groups assign them.

Why the sequence matters: **each circle assumes the inner circle knows better and follows its lead**. If employees aren't excited, outsiders think "they're closer to this than we are, so why would we be excited?" Worse, an early employee who feels bait-and-switched becomes an incredibly credible person in your own social circle who is now trying to ruin you.

**引用 / Quotes**

> "Out of the 1,000 facts in your head you're going to have to pick just between one and three."
> 「你脑子里有一千个事实，但你只能挑一到三个。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:19:33

> "Each circle is going to assume that the inner circle knows better than them and they're going to follow the lead of the inner circle."
> 「每一圈都会假设内圈比自己更懂，并跟随内圈。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:23:18

> "You can't skip a circle is my point."
> 「我的重点是：你不能跳过任何一圈。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:24:04

**案例 / Case Study: Substack 的产品更新如何传播**
中文：她用 Lenny 本人举例。Substack 每次发布新功能（比如"推荐"功能），Lenny 这种重度用户会比新作者更早知道，公司会确保他喜欢、甚至有点"参与感"。结果 Lenny 自发地推广了近一年——没人要求、没人付钱。原因是他有自己的动机：帮助其他写作者、展示社会证明。她指出：如果只是"Lenny，帮忙转一下"，他可能转一次就不再转了。同心圆里的"意见领袖"是 Substack 一号作者 Bill Bishop、增长最快的作者、报道媒体行业的记者，以及投资人。
English: She uses Lenny himself as the example. When Substack ships a feature (such as Recommendations), a power user like Lenny hears about it before a new writer does, and the company makes sure he loves it and feels a little invested. The result: Lenny organically promoted it for nearly a year, unasked and unpaid, because he had his own incentive (helping other writers, showing social proof). Had it been "Lenny, can you tweet this as a favor," he might have done it once and stopped. The Substack circles included Substacker #1 Bill Bishop, fast-growing writers, reporters who cover media, and investors.

**工具 / Tool: 一页 Google Doc**
中文：她说"大多数传播问题都能用一页 Google Doc 解决"。步骤：列出受众（5 到 6 个就够，再多是"第一世界的烦恼"）→ 按影响力和可信度排序 → 对每一圈写下他们的文化敏感带 → 写下他们"智力上住在哪里"（听哪些播客、去哪些线下会议、在哪些 subreddit、上不上 Hacker News）。
English: "Most comms problems can be solved with a one-page Google Doc." List your audiences (stop at five or six) → rank them by influence and credibility → for each, write down their cultural erogenous zones → write down where they "reside intellectually" (which podcasts, which conferences, which subreddits, Hacker News or not).

**呼应 / Echo in other interviews**：Pirate Wires 播客里她给 Anduril 总结的十条规则中，有一条就是"The Inner Circle Comes First & Win Over Tribal Elders"（先搞定内圈，赢得部落长老），还有"Start With The Ends"（从目标倒推）和"Act Like An Insurgent, Not An Incumbent"（像叛军而不是在位者那样行事）。（Pirate Wires, 2024-03-06，据公开目录）

## 5. 传播公式：从商业目标倒推
## 5. The Comms Equation: Work Backward from the Business Goal

**中文**
传播衡量的是"氛围"，很难像 OKR 一样量化，所以她尽量引入框架。这个公式的起点必须是**商业目标**，不是传播目标（"曝光量""爆款"都不算）：
1. 商业目标：例如营收增长 X。
2. 要达成目标，需要**哪些人**做**什么动作**（多少某类人买这双运动鞋）。
3. 这些人需要**相信什么**才会行动（我的脚现在不舒服；脚可以更舒服；这会改善生活；这项新技术是真的）。
4. 他们**在哪里**接收信息（关注谁、听什么播客、看什么报纸）。
5. 得出：把这条信息、通过这些渠道、传给这些人、附上这个行动号召。

这样至少能确保你在推动某件事，而不是"对着虚空说话"。

**English**
Comms measures "vibes," so it is hard to know if you're doing the right thing in a world of OKRs; hence she imposes frameworks. This one starts from a **business goal**, never a comms goal (impressions and virality don't count):
1. Business goal: e.g. grow revenue by X.
2. Which **people** need to take which **action** (this many of this type of person buying these sneakers).
3. What they need to **believe** to act (my feet aren't comfortable now; they could be; that would improve my life; this sneaker technology is real).
4. **Where** they receive information (which accounts, podcasts, papers).
5. Result: deliver this message to these people through these mediums with this call to action.

That way you are at least pushing something forward rather than "saying words into the ether."

**引用 / Quotes**

> "It assumes that you have a business goal... it's not get this many impressions or go viral."
> 「前提是你有一个商业目标……不是拿到多少曝光、不是爆红。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:29:06

> "What do they need to believe in order to take that action?"
> 「他们需要相信什么，才会采取那个行动？」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:30:07

> "It only matters if there is a direction attached to it."
> 「只有附带明确方向的传播才有意义。」
> — Invest Like the Best EP.389, 2024-09-24（据公开摘要）

**呼应 / Echo**：在 Uncapped with Jack Altman（2025-09）里她把这个思路进一步画成三个圆的交集：**Circle of Truth**（关于公司的事实）、**Circle of Relevance**（受众在乎的事）、**Circle of Strategic Value**（对商业目标有帮助的事）。可说的话在三者交集里。（据公开摘要）

## 6. 压强 = 力 ÷ 面积：缩小表面积
## 6. Pressure = Force ÷ Area: Shrink the Surface

**中文**
物理公式直接套用到传播上：同样的力，面积越小，压强越大。**别试图用所有东西打动所有人**。精确锁定对象，把信息削尖到一个点，正中他们的文化敏感带，同样的时间、金钱和信用就能产生更大的冲击。

她把它描述为一条光谱：一端是"成为一个人的终身伴侣"（极端精准），另一端是"世界和平是好事"（人人同意，但对谁都没意义）。创业公司应该站在"少数人"那一端：先选定死忠，把他们养起来，让他们把你带到下一圈。**先做一个小垄断**——在互联网上，分母是整个互联网，拿下 0.01% 就是一门好生意。

她还谈到"力"就是你投入的资源：钱、时间，有时候还有信用（"请相信我"是要花信用的）。与其花 100 万美元触达 100 万人，不如花 100 美元精准触达最重要的 100 个人，让他们成为你的信使。

**English**
Straight from physics: with the same force, a smaller area yields more pressure. **Don't try to appeal to everybody with everything.** Target exactly whom you're talking to, sharpen the message to a point, hit the bullseye of their erogenous zone, and the same effort, money and credibility produce more impact.

She describes a continuum: one extreme is "becoming the life partner of one person" (ultra targeting); the other is "world peace is good" (nobody disagrees, nobody cares). Startups should sit toward the few-people end: choose your diehards, foster them, let them carry you to the next circle. **Create a tiny monopoly first.** Online, the denominator is the whole internet; 0.01% of it is a great business.

"Force" is what you spend: dollars, hours, and sometimes credibility ("please trust me" draws it down). Instead of a million dollars to reach a million people, spend $100 to reach the 100 most important people and let them become your messengers.

**引用 / Quotes**

> "If you decrease the surface area then with the same amount of force you can apply more pressure."
> 「缩小面积，同样的力就能产生更大的压强。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:31:29

> "If you're just starting out, get 10 diehards and just spend all your time [on them]."
> 「刚起步时，找到 10 个死忠，把所有时间花在他们身上。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:37:24

> "It's a food coloring in the ocean kind of thing as opposed to food coloring in a cup."
> 「（面向所有人）就像把食用色素倒进海里，而不是倒进杯子里。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:37:24

> "You can't be remarkable if you're trying to appeal to so many people that you have to become the average of 500,000 people's tastes."
> 「如果你想讨好那么多人，以至于要变成五十万人口味的平均值，你就不可能出众。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:40:03

**案例 A / Case A: Balaji 的《The Network State》**
中文：Balaji 出书没走"纽约时报书评 + 出版社巡回"的传统路线，而是自建分发渠道：上触达死忠的播客，绝不为了讨好所有人而稀释信息，对"永远不会喜欢他的人"坦然接受。结果真粉自发传教，把书推上 Amazon 榜首——据她所知一分钱推广费都没花。她的警告：公司常因为受不了有人生气而去讨好反对者，结果真粉失去热情，"特别"变成了"温吞"。
English: Balaji skipped the traditional book tour and built his own distribution: podcasts that reach his diehards, no watering down the message, total comfort with the people who will never like him. His true fans evangelized and pushed the book to the top of Amazon, without (as far as she knows) paying anyone. Her warning: companies hate being disliked, so they appeal to detractors, and then their true fans lose passion because the special thing has gone "milquetoast."

**案例 B / Case B: NYX 彩妆——营销预算为零**
中文：她认为彩妆行业在"go direct"上领先所有人，因为他们很早就明白：没人为了"订阅一个彩妆品牌"而买化妆品，人们是因为某个博主或卡戴珊的眼皮好看才买。L'Oréal 旗下的 NYX 据她所知市场营销花费为零，所有钱和精力都投在一小群网红身上。一支最普通的黑色眼线笔，可以在全美 CVS 断货几周。彩妆公司还有一个优势：**快**。一个趋势出现，一小时内就跟上；传统公司走完审批流程，机会已经没了。
English: She thinks the makeup industry is ahead of everyone on going direct, because it realized early that nobody buys makeup to subscribe to a brand; they buy because an influencer or a Kardashian has a nice eyelid. NYX (a L'Oréal division) spends, as far as she knows, $0 on marketing and pours everything into a small roster of influencers. A totally fungible black eyeliner sells out at CVS nationwide for weeks. Makeup companies are also **fast**: on a trend within an hour, while traditional companies route through approvals and miss it.

> "Watch makeup companies, watch consumer companies, they're doing it right, they speak with a human voice, they speak through human beings and they're fast."
> 「看看彩妆公司、看看消费品公司，他们做得对：用人的声音说话、通过真人说话，而且快。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:50:25

**呼应 / Echo：同一个公式用于防守**
中文：在 Knowledge Project（2025-09）里，她把同一个公式反过来用在"被攻击"时：你改变不了对方的力有多大，但你可以改变自己暴露的面积。
English: On The Knowledge Project (Sept 2025) she flips the same formula for defense:
> "If you're trying to relieve pressure, you don't get to change how much force is coming at you, but you can change the surface area."
> 「想减压时，你无法改变冲过来的力有多大，但你可以改变自己的表面积。」
> — The Knowledge Project "How To Build A Cult"（据公开摘要）

## 7. 敢冒险："作为"的错误好过"不作为"的错误
## 7. Take Risks: Mistakes of Commission Beat Mistakes of Omission

**中文**
创业公司最大的敌人是**现状**。"什么都不做"看起来最安全，实际上是让现状默认获胜。她鼓励犯"作为"的错误（mistakes of commission）而不是"不作为"的错误（mistakes of omission）：前者立刻可观察、可学习、可快速调整；后者你什么都学不到，甚至注意不到机会溜走。

她的类比：拿着现金 vs. 投进市场。现金看起来不会亏，但世界在前进、市场在增长，你实际上在变穷。投资会有波动，但长期好得多。

**English**
A startup's enemy is the **status quo**. Doing nothing feels safest, but it lets the status quo win by default. She urges mistakes of commission over mistakes of omission: the former you can observe, learn from, and correct fast; the latter teach you nothing and you may not even notice opportunities slipping by.

Her analogy: sitting in cash vs. investing. Cash feels safe, but the world moves, the market grows, and you get poorer in real terms. Investing is volatile but far better over the long run.

**引用 / Quotes**

> "If you're a startup your enemy is the status quo... the best way to minimize risk is to do nothing. You're letting the status quo win."
> 「如果你是创业公司，你的敌人是现状……最小化风险的最好办法就是什么都不做，而这等于让现状获胜。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:11:34

> "I always encourage people to try to make mistakes of commission rather than omission."
> 「我总是鼓励大家去犯'作为'的错误，而不是'不作为'的错误。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:11:34

**案例 / Case Study: 产假期间发的 Substack 言论自由推文串**
中文：这件事的风险有三层：话题本身已经让很多人愤怒；她在产假中、状态不佳，有执行风险（事实证明里面确实有一条"胡说八道"）；以及"本来没事，你非要冒头搞出事"的风险——如果搞砸了让公司难堪、让创始人 Chris、Hamish、Jairaj 不高兴，那就是她的失职。但创始人支持了她，而最可能在 Substack 写作的人恰恰认同这个立场，于是风险得到了回报。
English: The risk had three layers: the topic already had a lot of people angry; she was on leave and "addled," so there was execution risk (one tweet did turn out nonsensical); and the risk of "poking your head up and making a thing where there wasn't a thing." Had it embarrassed the company or upset the founders (Chris, Hamish, Jairaj), that would have been a failure of her job. But the founders were supportive, and the people most likely to write on Substack appreciated the stance, so the risk paid off.

**呼应 / Echo：Wired 的更正**
中文：据 Fortune/Yahoo Finance 报道，Wired 曾暗示 Substack"付钱给极端分子"，她连发推文指出记者的信息有误导性，最终该报道发布了更正。这是"敢于公开反击"的另一个实例。
English: Per Fortune (via Yahoo Finance), when Wired suggested Substack "paid extremists," she fired off tweets calling the reporting misleading, which eventually led to a correction. Another instance of being willing to push back publicly.

## 8. 用人的方式说话，并且只选一个主渠道
## 8. Speak as a Human, and Pick One Primary Channel

**中文**
选渠道的原则：**选发言人最擅长、最像自己的那种媒介**，然后把所有资源砸进去，做起来再扩展。如果东西是别人代笔的，别人看得出来。
- Elon Musk 天生适合 Twitter，你无法想象他写长篇深思的博客。
- Brian Armstrong、Hamish McKenzie、Chris Best 写真诚有效的长文，比只发推更好。
- 同时做 Instagram、Twitter、LinkedIn、YouTube、TikTok 六七个渠道，哪个都做不起来。

她还有几个反直觉的判断：
- **不是每个创始人都必须上 Twitter**。但如果你是使命驱动型（必然有人爱有人恨，你需要为自己的事辩护），或者创始人魅力是招聘的核心（Anduril 的 Palmer Luckey 对工程师有磁性），那就必须在场。
- **LinkedIn 被严重低估**：流量和停留时间巨大，但 95%（她自己估计）的内容是互相祝贺周年，所以"有趣内容的竞争集合 vs. 注意力总量"的比例极好。职业相关的东西在 LinkedIn 效果更好，而且不像 Twitter 那样一言不合就在评论区打起来。
- 选定渠道不代表永远绑定，可以随着舒适度变化而换，别因为选择困难而瘫痪。

**English**
The channel rule: **pick the medium your spokesperson is naturally best at**, invest everything there, build it up, then expand. Ghostwritten content shows.
- Elon Musk was born to do Twitter; you can't picture him writing long thoughtful blog posts.
- Brian Armstrong, Hamish McKenzie and Chris Best write sincere, effective long-form, which beats them doing only tweets.
- Trying to build six or seven channels at once gets you nowhere.

Some counterintuitive calls:
- **Not every founder needs Twitter.** But if you're mission-driven (some will love you, some will hate you, and you'll need to defend the thing), or if founder charisma drives recruiting (Palmer Luckey at Anduril is magnetic to engineers), you must be there.
- **LinkedIn is underutilized**: huge eyeballs and time, but 95% (her own estimate) of the content is work-anniversary congratulations, so the ratio of interesting content to attention is excellent. Career-related content does better there, and it's not a "cesspool" where everything turns into a fight.
- Picking one channel isn't forever; founders get more comfortable on camera after reps. Don't let choice paralysis stop you from starting.

**引用 / Quotes**

> "If somebody else is ghost-writing all their stuff, it shows."
> 「如果都是别人代笔，是看得出来的。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:43:55

> "Pick the thing that your spokesperson is the best at and then invest everything into that channel."
> 「选你的发言人最擅长的那件事，然后把所有资源投进那个渠道。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:44:53

> "LinkedIn is super underutilized because it gets a ton of eyeballs and time, but most of the content sucks."
> 「LinkedIn 被严重低估，因为它有海量的眼球和时长，但大部分内容很烂。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:48:14

**呼应 / Echo**：在 Knowledge Project 里她把这一点推到极致："邪教领袖不会把信息交给一个'非常体面、专业、正常'的人去讲以避免争议。"最有效的传播者就是组织的真正领导者本人。（据公开摘要）

## 9. 建立自己的受众：三步走，稳定压倒爆款
## 9. Build Your Own Audience: Three Steps, Consistency Over Virality

**中文**
1. **评估**：你擅长什么、享受什么（长文？播客？短视频？）。
2. **建号并准备弹药**：像发 Substack 一样，上线前先备好一两周的内容。TikTok 等算法在新账号起步阶段会给你倾斜，要"连续出拳"抓住这个窗口。别发一条就等。
3. **持续的内容策略**：明确受众和他们在乎什么，定下节奏、社区互动方式和公告方式。规律和一致性是增长的核心，和做 newsletter 一样。

常见错误是"偶尔想搞一个爆款"而不是持续输出。为爆款而爆款的内容（比如截图 iPhone 六位密码界面配文"苹果最好的功能"）很容易被看穿，也带不来会持续关注你的人。**错配的受众比没有受众更糟**：一条仓鼠推文爆了，来的人都在等仓鼠内容，数字涨了但毫无意义。

她坦承自己不是好榜样：消失一两周，然后一天发一堆。"这不是最佳实践。"

**English**
1. **Assess**: what are you good at and what do you enjoy (long-form, podcast, short video)?
2. **Set up and load the pipeline**: as with launching a Substack, have a week or two of content ready. Algorithms (TikTok especially) favor new accounts that come out strong and regular, so ride that window. Don't post once and wait.
3. **Ongoing content strategy**: know whom you're reaching and what they care about; set the cadence, the community-management approach, and how you'll do announcements. Regularity and consistency are the engine of growth, just like a newsletter.

The common mistake is trying to go viral every once in a while instead of being consistent. Viral bait (screenshotting the iPhone passcode screen with "best feature Apple ever built") is obvious and doesn't earn followers who expect unique value from you. **A mismatched audience is worse than none**: a viral hamster tweet brings people who expect hamster content; the number goes up but means nothing.

She admits she's a bad example: she disappears for two weeks, then posts a bunch in one day. "It's not a best practice."

**引用 / Quotes**

> "A mistake that people make I think is just every once in a while trying to go viral as opposed to just being consistent."
> 「大家常犯的错误是偶尔想搞一个爆款，而不是持续稳定地输出。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:55:42

> "It's just making the number go up, which is not that meaningful."
> 「那只是让数字涨上去，没什么意义。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 00:58:03

**呼应 / Echo：Knowledge Project 的"信任工程"**
中文：她认为信任可以被"工程化"，由三个要素构成：反复出现（从陌生人变成熟人）、共享价值观、传递信念（conviction）。这正是"持续输出"背后的机制。她还给出了优先级排序：**先是钩子（hook），然后是怎么讲，最后才是在哪里讲**。钩子是最被忽视的一环。（The Knowledge Project, 2025-09，据公开摘要）
English: She argues trust can be engineered from three elements: repeated exposure (becoming familiar instead of a stranger), shared values, and conveyed conviction. That is the mechanism behind consistency. She also ranks what matters: **the hook first, then how you tell the story, then where you tell it.** The hook is the most overlooked part. (The Knowledge Project, Sept 2025, per public summary)

## 11. 行动清单（可直接照做）
## 11. Action Checklist

**中文**
1. 写一页 Google Doc：列 5 到 6 个受众圈，排序，写下每圈的文化敏感带和他们"住在哪里"。
2. 从 1000 个事实里挑 1 到 3 个，写成二年级小学生能懂的一句话，去掉陈词滥调，加一个画面或类比。
3. 先说服员工，再说服下一圈。永远不要跳圈。
4. 从商业目标倒推：谁、做什么、需要相信什么、在哪里触达。
5. 缩小表面积：先拿下 10 个死忠，做一个"小垄断"。
6. 创始人本人、第一人称、真人口吻发声。选一个最擅长的渠道，只做这一个。
7. 上线前备好一两周内容，然后保持节奏。不追爆款。
8. 敢于犯"作为"的错误。平时就说话，别等危机。
9. 把产品免费送给"会痴迷它 ∩ 在目标人群中有影响力"的那一小撮人。
10. 检查每一条对外信息：是否只有自己懂？是否像公司公告？是否只是想涨数字？

**English**
1. Write the one-page Google Doc: 5–6 audience circles, ranked, each with erogenous zones and where they reside.
2. Pick 1–3 facts out of 1,000; write a second-grader sentence; strip clichés; add an image or analogy.
3. Win employees first, then the next circle. Never skip a circle.
4. Work backward from the business goal: who, what action, what belief, which channel.
5. Shrink the surface area: get 10 diehards, build a tiny monopoly.
6. Founder speaks, first person, human voice. One channel you're best at; only that one.
7. Load a week or two of content before launch, then keep the cadence. Don't chase virality.
8. Make mistakes of commission. Talk in peacetime so you can talk in a crisis.
9. Give the product away free to the sliver of people who will be obsessed with it and have reach among your target audience.
10. Audit every outbound message: inside joke? corporate decree? number-go-up bait?

> 她的最后一条建议 / Her closing tip:
> "Give it away for free to the right people... whoever falls into that sliver of the Venn diagram, shower them with free product."
> 「把它免费送给对的人……落在维恩图那一小片交集里的人，用免费产品把他们淹没。」
> — Lenny's Podcast, 01:01:37
